The five factors that should drive the decision

1. Campaign production should fit your team

The best editor is the one your team can use consistently without creating a design bottleneck. Test the full production sequence: drafting, layout, reusable content, mobile preview, approvals, test sends, scheduling, and reporting.

MailerLite's drag-and-drop newsletter editor may appeal to businesses that want to produce campaigns without a specialist designer. However, the editor alone should not determine the purchase. Check whether either platform can preserve your brand system, support the necessary approval process, and produce accessible emails efficiently.

2. Signup capture is part of the product, not an add-on

Audience growth depends on what happens before the first email. List every place where someone can subscribe: the company website, campaign landing pages, checkout, events, retail locations, and lead magnets.

MailerLite can cover several of these touchpoints through forms, pop-ups, landing pages, and its iPad subscriber app. That consolidated approach may reduce the number of tools a small team must maintain. Before selecting it, confirm that the chosen plan provides the required publishing controls, fields, consent options, and capacity.

Apply the same test to Brevo. A platform that appears less expensive can become more costly if the business must add a separate form builder, landing-page service, or integration layer.

3. Automation depth matters more than template quantity

Prebuilt templates can accelerate setup, but a business should evaluate automation with a real workflow. A useful trial scenario might include:

  1. A subscriber joins through a specific form.
  2. The system applies the correct audience classification.
  3. A welcome message is sent.
  4. A delay or engagement condition controls the next step.
  5. The subscriber follows a different path based on an action or attribute.
  6. The team can understand and revise the workflow later.

MailerLite offers automation templates and activity-triggered emails in addition to general marketing automation. Confirm that your required triggers and actions are included in the plan you are considering. For Brevo, recreate the same scenario rather than accepting a generic statement that automation is available.

4. Integrations determine operational reliability

An integration logo does not prove that a connection will support your workflow. Define which system owns subscriber data, consent status, purchase history, segments, and unsubscribe records. Then establish how quickly data moves, what happens when synchronization fails, and who is responsible for troubleshooting.

MailerLite identifies Shopify, WooCommerce, and Stripe among its integrations and also offers developer resources. Businesses considering custom work should review the relevant API documentation before purchase. Do the equivalent technical review for Brevo, including plan requirements and usage limits, if API access or custom integration is important.

5. Cost must reflect growth, not just today's list

Compare the platforms using at least three scenarios: your current audience, a realistic 12-month forecast, and a higher-growth case. Include every cost driver that affects the planned workflow, such as contact or subscriber count, sending volume, user access, automation availability, landing pages, and paid add-ons.

Also account for switching and operating costs. A slightly higher software bill may be reasonable if it replaces another tool or reduces manual work. Conversely, a broad platform is poor value when the team will use only basic campaigns and forms. Check current plans and terms directly before making a budget decision.

Where MailerLite may fit better

MailerLite deserves a place on the shortlist when a small team wants email campaigns, audience capture, landing pages, and automation within one environment. It is particularly relevant when the business also wants to explore adjacent workflows such as a blog, paid newsletter, digital products, booking, or ecommerce connections.

Creators and lean marketing teams may also value having templates, educational resources, integrations, and developer documentation available around the same platform. The benefit depends on whether those capabilities are included at an acceptable plan level and can replace tools already in the stack.

Where Brevo may be the better choice

Brevo may be the stronger choice if its current offering scores higher on the requirements that matter most to your business. That conclusion should come from a documented plan comparison and a workflow demonstration, not brand familiarity.

Give Brevo additional weight if it produces a better result for your projected database, sending pattern, required channels, team structure, or integration architecture. Ask for written confirmation of any capability that is essential to launch, particularly when it affects automation, data portability, access controls, or total cost.

Implementation questions to answer before signing

  • Data migration: Which contacts, fields, consent records, suppression lists, segments, and templates must move?
  • Domain setup: What authentication work is required, and who will control the necessary DNS changes?
  • Permissions: Can the business assign appropriate access to writers, approvers, administrators, and outside contractors?
  • Compliance: Can the planned setup support the consent, retention, deletion, and unsubscribe processes required by the business?
  • Security: Are the vendor's current security documentation, data-processing terms, access controls, and incident procedures acceptable?
  • Portability: Can contacts, relevant fields, suppression data, and reports be exported in usable formats?
  • Support: Does the selected plan provide a support route and response expectation suitable for campaign-critical issues?
  • Ownership: Who will maintain forms, automations, integrations, templates, and data quality after launch?

Final recommendation

MailerLite may fit a small business that wants to consolidate campaigns, signup capture, landing pages, and automation, especially when its website, commerce, booking, or creator-oriented tools can remove other software from the stack.

Brevo should win the decision when its current plan performs better against your actual usage model and critical workflows. Do not select either platform until the team has built one campaign, one acquisition path, and one representative automation, then calculated the cost at expected growth. The platform that completes those tasks with acceptable complexity, governance, and long-term cost is the more defensible choice.
