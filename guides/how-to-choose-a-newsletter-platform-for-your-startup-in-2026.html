Which platform should a startup choose?

A. Media or newsletter-first startup

Why: the publication itself is an acquisition asset or product, so the public site, growth, and monetization model matter alongside email. Beehiiv belongs on the shortlist because its product covers newsletters, websites, and monetization features. Tradeoff: confirm which plan includes the capabilities you need and model subscriber-tier pricing. Shortlist: Beehiiv, then verify any required workflow directly.

B. SaaS or product-led startup

Why: product education and lead nurturing may depend on forms and automated sequences. Kit Free includes unlimited forms and landing pages, unlimited broadcasts, digital products and subscriptions, and API access. Tradeoff: visual automations and sequences require a paid plan. Shortlist: Kit, after confirming the exact product-data connections you need.

C. Founder-led publication

Why: a founder building a direct audience may value a straightforward writing and publishing routine. Substack is worth evaluating when its simple model fits. Tradeoff: paid subscriptions carry a 10% Substack fee plus separate Stripe processing. Shortlist: Substack for simplicity; compare Beehiiv or Kit if growth tools or product workflows become central.

D. Paid newsletter business

Why: platform fees affect subscription margins. Beehiiv states it takes 0% of paid-subscription revenue; Substack takes 10% plus processing. Kit supports digital products and subscriptions, but confirm applicable payment terms. Tradeoff: compare plan prices, processing, subscriber limits, and required tools- not just platform share. Shortlist: compare Beehiiv and Substack economics against your assumptions; include Kit when products or automation are central.

Substack for startups

Where it may fit: a founder or writer who wants to publish without a publishing subscription fee and values a simple publication model. Substack says publishing is free regardless of subscriber count.

Cost boundary: when charging for subscriptions, Substack takes 10%, and Stripe processing fees apply separately. Its help page gives a U.S. card example of 2.9% + $0.30 per payment. This is the cited U.S. example, not a universal rate.

Pros: free publishing at any subscriber count; a straightforward starting model. Tradeoffs: paid subscriptions incur the 10% platform fee plus processing. If the startup needs more control over a broader business workflow, compare other options against those requirements.

Before you commit: 5 checks for a startup

  1. Portability: confirm subscriber/contact exports, content export availability, and what happens to your domain and URLs if you move.
  2. Consent and privacy: check opt-in records, unsubscribe handling, and privacy requirements that apply to your startup.
  3. Deliverability controls: verify domain authentication and sender-reputation tools. This article does not compare vendor deliverability; evaluate your own requirements and evidence.
  4. Integrations and workflow: list required product, CRM, or API connections and verify the exact workflow before purchasing.
  5. Support and migration: confirm import needs and the level of setup support your team requires.
