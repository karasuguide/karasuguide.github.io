Looking for a replacement for AWeber usually means one of three things: a creator-focused publishing workflow, deeper store or sales automation, or a different cost model. This comparison names seven real products and highlights what each one does well, where its published plan limits matter, and when it may fit better than AWeber. We reviewed the vendors’ official product and plan pages; we did not conduct hands-on product testing.

How the seven products differ

1. Kit — creator newsletters and audience monetization

Best for: Writers, educators, and independent creators who publish directly to an audience. Kit’s Free plan includes forms and landing pages, broadcasts, tagging and segmentation, digital products, and subscriptions. The important catch is that visual automations and email sequences require a paid plan. Free supports up to 10,000 subscribers; paid pricing varies with subscriber count and plan.

Why choose it over AWeber: Consider Kit when creator recommendations, newsletter publishing, and selling digital products are the main workflow. AWeber may be a better fit if you want its established combination of broadcasts, forms, landing pages, and automations. Kit’s official Free-plan details.

2. MailerLite — a compact email and website toolkit

Best for: A small business that wants newsletters, sign-up forms, landing pages, and a modest web presence in one account. The current Free plan allows 250 active subscribers and 2,500 emails per month, plus up to 3 automations, 3 forms, 1 landing page, and 1 website of up to 5 pages. Paid plans use subscriber tiers and raise the feature and sending limits.

Why choose it over AWeber: It can suit a team that values a small website and email workflow in the same tool. The 250-subscriber free ceiling is restrictive for a growing list, so compare the paid tier at your actual audience size. MailerLite’s official Free-plan limits and pricing page.

3. Brevo — send-volume pricing and multiple channels

Best for: Teams that want email with adjacent customer channels such as SMS, WhatsApp, or chat, or that store more contacts than they email each month. Brevo’s Free plan allows up to 100,000 stored contacts but only 300 email sends per day; unused daily sends do not roll over. Paid tiers are selected around monthly email volume, and contact storage can also depend on the selected tier.

Why choose it over AWeber: Brevo is worth comparing when a large database does not translate into frequent sends and multi-channel communication is useful. Model the daily cap, monthly volume, and any add-ons before comparing the subscription cost. Brevo’s official plan and pricing details.

4. Mailchimp — general-purpose small-business campaigns

Best for: Small businesses that want a broad campaign platform and familiar audience-management tools. Its Free plan is capped at 250 contacts, 500 monthly sends, and 250 sends per day. If the free contact or send limit is exceeded, Mailchimp pauses sending until the account is upgraded or the contact total is reduced. Paid cost depends on the contact tier and selected plan.

Why choose it over AWeber: Compare Mailchimp if its broader marketing toolkit and existing integrations match your stack. Count billable contacts carefully, including how the plan treats contacts you do not actively email. Mailchimp’s official plan limits and plan comparison.

5. Omnisend — ecommerce lifecycle marketing

Best for: Online stores that want campaigns and automated messages to respond to shopping and order activity. Omnisend lists prebuilt ecommerce workflows such as cart-abandonment, welcome, post-purchase, and re-engagement flows. Its Free plan covers up to 250 contacts and 500 emails per month; paid plans scale with billable contacts and sending volume. SMS costs and plan availability depend on the selected tier and destination.

Why choose it over AWeber: Choose it for closer alignment with store-focused lifecycle workflows, not simply because it also sends email. A publication or service business may not need ecommerce-specific automation. Omnisend’s official plan comparison.

6. ActiveCampaign — deeper automation with sales context

Best for: A small team coordinating segmented email journeys with sales or ecommerce data. ActiveCampaign’s current plan page lists marketing automation and integrations across its tiers; its CRM capabilities and add-ons depend on plan. The Starter tier is limited to 5 actions per automation and one user. Pricing is selected by plan and contact count, so use the live calculator rather than relying on an old quoted price.

Why choose it over AWeber: It is a candidate when you need multi-step automation and customer context that goes beyond newsletter broadcasts. The added workflow and account complexity is only worthwhile if someone on the team will own it. ActiveCampaign’s official plan and feature comparison.

7. GetResponse — webinars, courses, and email under one roof

Best for: Educators and creator businesses that use email to fill webinars, deliver courses, or sell paid newsletters. GetResponse’s plans are tiered by list size. Starter has one automation workflow; the Creator plan includes webinars for up to 100 attendees, online courses, and content-monetization tools. Webinar and team options may be paid add-ons on lower plans, so check the current plan comparison for your region and billing term.

Why choose it over AWeber: Compare it when webinar or course delivery is part of the same customer journey. If you only send newsletters and simple follow-ups, those extra modules may add cost without enough value. GetResponse’s official plan breakdown and pricing page.

When AWeber may still be the better choice

AWeber remains a reasonable shortlist option when you want email broadcasts, signup forms, landing pages, and automation without making ecommerce, CRM, or webinar functionality the center of the purchase. Its Free plan is limited to 500 subscribers, 3,000 monthly emails, one list profile, and one workflow automation; Lite and Plus have different item limits and prices. Confirm the plan that fits your list and workflow directly in AWeber Free-plan details and on its pricing page. The existing AWeber offer remains available for readers who want to review it; this article does not claim that any alternative is universally better.

A practical way to make the decision

  1. Write down one job to replace. For example: weekly newsletter publishing, a welcome sequence, abandoned-cart recovery, or lead follow-up.
  2. Use your real audience numbers. Compare active and billable contacts, monthly sends, users, and required workflow actions against the plan limits above.
  3. Check the specific integration and data path. Confirm which fields, events, opt-outs, and suppression records move between your website, store, CRM, and email tool.
  4. Estimate switching cost. Include rebuilding forms, landing pages, templates, automations, and reports—not only the subscription.
  5. Test before committing. Use a trial or a limited internal workflow to verify the messages, links, permissions, and unsubscribe path. This guide is based on official published information, not hands-on testing of these products.
