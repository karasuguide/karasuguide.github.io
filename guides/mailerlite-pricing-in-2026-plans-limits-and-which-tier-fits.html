What does MailerLite cost?

The Free plan costs $0. The official pricing page currently lists Comfort from $12/month and Power from $25/month. These are starting prices, not fixed prices for every subscriber tier. MailerLite’s plan-and-billing documentation says Comfort and Power are offered in monthly and annual billing, and the annual option carries a 10% discount. The live pricing selector controls the subscriber tier, currency, and billing option; verify the exact amount there before comparing quotes.

MailerLite states that paid-plan pricing is based on usage, including subscriber count and sending volume. The static public pricing page does not provide a complete, fixed dollar table for every tier. This guide therefore gives the verified starting prices and the limits needed to compare plans, but does not estimate prices for subscriber tiers whose displayed amounts have not been independently confirmed.

Applicable VAT or other taxes may be added at checkout. MailerLite says billing currency is selected based on billing address, with EUR and USD supported. Annual plans renew automatically unless canceled, and MailerLite says annual billing saves 10%. The current pricing page allows switching between monthly and yearly display. Confirm the selected option, currency, tax, discount, and renewal total in checkout.

How subscriber limits are counted

MailerLite counts unique contacts with Active status. Unsubscribed and bounced contacts do not count, and the same active address is not counted twice just because it appears in more than one group or segment. However, its billing guidance says the count is cumulative within a billing cycle: an address that was Active at any point in that cycle can continue to count even if it is deleted later.

For a Free account, the limit is 250 active subscribers. If the account exceeds the limit, campaign and automation sending, as well as adding subscribers, can be paused until the account returns under the limit or upgrades. MailerLite says Free accounts are not charged automatically for crossing the limit; upgrading is a manual choice.

How monthly email sending limits work

A send is one email delivered to one subscriber. A campaign sent to 500 people uses 500 sends from the monthly allowance. Automation emails also count. Free includes up to 2,500 sends per 30-day cycle. Comfort includes 10 times the ceiling of its selected subscriber tier. For example, MailerLite’s published example gives 50,000 sends for the 5,000-subscriber tier. Power is described as unlimited, subject to fair use. Enterprise sending volume is custom.

Free usage resets on the account registration anniversary; paid usage resets on the billing date. Check your actual cycle before planning a large campaign. For Comfort, reaching the sending limit can trigger an automatic move to a higher tier. MailerLite says an account moved up only because it exceeded sends may return to the subscriber tier that fits at renewal. Subscriber-count upgrades can also change the amount charged. Review the account notice and billing page if usage approaches a limit.

Feature limits that can affect your plan choice

  • Free: up to 3 active automations, 3 forms, 1 landing page, 1 website of up to 5 pages, and 1 digital product.
  • Comfort: up to 50 active automations, 10 forms, 10 websites and landing pages combined, and 5 digital products. The plan also lists 3 user seats.
  • Power: unlimited feature use and user seats.
  • Enterprise: custom plan and sending volume for larger lists, with dedicated support options.

Feature limits count active or published items, not drafts. Reaching a feature cap does not necessarily move an account to a higher plan automatically. MailerLite says Comfort users over a feature limit cannot create, activate, or edit affected items until they reduce usage or upgrade. Free accounts that exceed relevant limits can enter a restricted state that also blocks sending and subscriber imports. Check the current plan comparison for feature-specific details before relying on a capability.

Comfort vs. Power: which paid plan should you compare?

Comfort may fit a team whose list and sending needs fit a paid subscriber tier and whose active automations, forms, sites, and digital products stay within the stated caps. It offers a defined monthly sending allowance tied to the tier and up to three user seats.

Power is the plan to compare when Comfort’s feature caps or sending allowance would constrain the intended workflow. MailerLite lists unlimited feature use, unlimited user seats, and unlimited monthly sends subject to fair use. Whether the additional headroom is worth the higher price depends on the exact subscriber tier and expected use.

Enterprise is aimed at lists above 200,000 subscribers and uses custom sending volume and pricing. A public fixed price is not listed; request a quote for the specific audience and requirements.

Billing details to check before subscribing

  • Choose monthly or annual billing and compare the total commitment, not only a displayed monthly equivalent.
  • Check the subscriber tier and sending allowance selected in the pricing calculator.
  • Budget for tax and confirm the currency at checkout.
  • Understand automatic tier changes if subscriber count or sends exceed a paid limit.
  • Annual subscriptions renew automatically unless canceled; review the renewal date and renewal amount.
  • Discounts and coupons may have conditions and may not combine. Confirm eligibility and the final checkout total.

What changed for existing MailerLite customers in 2026?

MailerLite introduced its current pricing structure on June 16, 2026. Its transition guidance says the former Growing Business plan is renamed Comfort and Advanced is renamed Power. Existing paid accounts move to the new structure at renewal or when the account holder manually upgrades or downgrades. A legacy account may therefore see different plan names or timing before transition. Check the account’s own Plan and billing page rather than assuming every subscriber already has identical terms.

MailerLite’s Free plan also changed on June 16, 2026: the active-subscriber limit moved from 500 to 250, monthly sending moved from 12,000 to 2,500, and the user allowance became two seats. Older reviews or pricing articles may describe the earlier limits, so check their update dates carefully.

Is MailerLite’s free plan enough?

Free may be enough for a small list of up to 250 active subscribers and no more than 2,500 emails per 30-day cycle, provided the workflow fits the feature caps. It includes two user seats and limited forms, automations, website, landing page, and digital-product use. It is not a safe assumption that sending will continue after a cap is crossed: MailerLite says some account functions can be paused or restricted until usage is reduced or the account is upgraded.
