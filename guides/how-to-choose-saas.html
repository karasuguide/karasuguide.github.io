How to choose SaaS without overbuying

Feature lists make almost every software product look essential. A better buying process starts with the job you need done and works backward to the smallest tool that reliably solves it.

Define the job

Write one sentence describing the outcome you need. Avoid vague goals such as “improve productivity.” Prefer concrete outcomes such as “automatically route form submissions to the correct sales owner.”

Compare total cost

Look beyond the headline monthly price. Check user limits, usage limits, required add-ons, annual commitments, implementation time, and the cost of moving away later.

Check integrations

A tool that fits your existing stack can be more valuable than a more powerful tool that creates another data silo. Verify native integrations and API access before committing.

Test the exit

Check whether you can export your data in a useful format and whether important workflows can be recreated elsewhere. Portability reduces long-term lock-in risk.

Buy for the next year, not the next decade

Small teams rarely need enterprise complexity on day one. Choose for your current workflow plus a reasonable amount of growth.
